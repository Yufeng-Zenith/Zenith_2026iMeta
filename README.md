# Milk-tripeptide-alleviates-colitis-through-microbiota-driven-aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor-activation
Code and metadata for milk tripeptide colitis microbiota study

This study demonstrates that the milk-derived bioactive tripeptide Val-Pro-Pro is an effective substance for ameliorating dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced experimental colitis in mice. Administration of Val-Pro-Pro preserves the gut microbial community and enriches specific commensal bacteria, notably Dubosiella newyorkensis. We identify that D. newyorkensis produces a key tryptophan metabolite, 5-methoxy-2-methylindole-3-acetic acid (MMIA), which alleviates experimental colitis. Biochemical and structural studies identify MMIA as a novel agonist of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR). By activating AHR, MMIA downregulates the iron uptake–associated proteins divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1) and duodenal cytochrome B (DCYTB), reduces intracellular labile free iron, and counteracts ferroptosis in intestinal epithelial cells to alleviate colitis. Genetic AHR deficiency attenuates MMIA-mediated protection against colitis, with pharmacological findings providing supporting evidence for AHR involvement. These findings reveal a previously unrecognized beneficial commensal bacterium–metabolite–AHR–ferroptosis axis that underpins the anti-colitis activity of milk-derived bioactive peptides, offering a promising dietary strategy for the management of ulcerative colitis (UC). This repository provides the analysis scripts and pipelines used in the study for data integration, statistical analysis, functional association analysis, and figure reproduction.
